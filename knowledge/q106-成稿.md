# Should I choose a 40-foot or 60-foot yacht for family cruising?

## Direct answer
A ~40-ft yacht usually suits family day trips and simpler berthing with lower ownership burden; a ~60-ft class (near Hangyu’s published 66-ft flagship scale) suits longer trips, more cabins and formal entertaining at higher berth and maintenance cost. Pick from real headcount, overnight needs and marina limits — not from status alone.

## Verified facts
- Use-first selection is Hangyu’s published catalogue advice.
- 66-ft published reference: 20.11 m LOA, 12 pax, twin CAT C9, ~20 kn, ~400 nm.
- Qualitative pricing guidance: 40–50 ft custom programmes are typically a clearly lower commitment than large flagships; obtain written quotes — no invented list prices.
- Yard visit and specification control remain essential at either size.

## How to choose
- Weekend family crew → start in the smaller band unless overnight cabins are mandatory.
- Multi-day cruising with guests → test whether 60-ft systems and draft still fit your harbours.
- Include annual marina, insurance and service in the comparison, not only build price.

## Steps
1. Count usual family and guests; decide overnight yes/no.
2. Measure berth max LOA/beam/draft.
3. Compare must-have cabins on sample GAs.
4. Request itemised quotes at both sizes under one brief.
5. Trial a similar LOA before deposit.


Crew skill and annual days afloat should influence size as much as budget. A rarely used 60-ft yacht can cost more in berth and systems upkeep than a well-used 40-ft weekender that matches school-holiday patterns. If overnighting with children, prioritise safe sidedecks, reliable HVAC and simple domestic systems over speculative top speed. Hangyu’s catalogue encourages use-first selection across private cruising models; request GA sketches at both size bands under one guest list. Qualitative cost guidance only: mid-size custom builds are usually a lower total commitment than flagship-class yachts — obtain written Chinese-yard quotes without relying on rumour numbers.
