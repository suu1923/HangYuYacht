# Aluminum vs fiberglass yachts: which is better for coastal cruising?

## Direct answer
Neither material wins for every coastal programme. Fibreglass often fits leisure styling, fairing quality and series moulds; aluminium often fits impact tolerance, structural repairability and weight-sensitive or high-frequency use. Choose against waters, size, maintenance skill and the builder’s proven process — Hangyu can discuss both routes as a multi-material Chinese builder.

## Verified facts
- Fibreglass: flexible styling and good marine durability; assess laminate quality, moulds and repair access.
- Aluminium: low weight and practical repair; control welding, galvanic corrosion, insulation and noise.
- Hangyu publicly covers FRP leisure/business yachts and aluminium multihulls among other paths.
- Final material choice belongs in the project specification and certificates.

## How to choose
- Owner-cruiser focus on finish and accommodation → scrutinise FRP tooling and interior systems.
- Harder commercial duty or repair logistics → scrutinise aluminium procedures.
- Demand equal equipment and survey assumptions when comparing quotations.

## Steps
1. Document coastal routes and annual hours.
2. Ask for material-specific maintenance notes.
3. Verify yard capability for the chosen material.
4. Inspect workmanship samples.
5. Contract material standards and inspection hold points.


Coastal cruising still spans sheltered bays and more exposed passages; write the worst regular route into the brief. Fibreglass series boats may deliver faster cosmetic completion when moulds exist; aluminium one-offs may win when impact risk or repair logistics dominate. Insulation and acoustic treatment matter for aluminium interiors; osmotic and laminate QA matter for FRP. Never treat website speed or range as contractual without trial definitions. Document the expected annual haul-out cycle and local yard skills for aluminium welding versus FRP repair when you score the options. Share the same coastal itinerary with both material proposals so quotations stay comparable.
