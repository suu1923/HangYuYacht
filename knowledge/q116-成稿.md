# What should a tourism operator consider when choosing a passenger sightseeing boat?

## Direct answer
Operators should lock route and waterway status, approved passenger capacity, timetable intensity, boarding flow, lifesaving/fire scope, accessibility, energy cost, cleaning access and the local approval path before choosing a hull. Sightseeing success is compliance and uptime first, styling second. Hangyu can discuss tourism/passenger directions once the operating boundary is clear.

## Verified facts
- Catalogue tourism priorities: compliant capacity, viewing efficiency, passenger flow, durability, cost and cleaning.
- Approvals vary; confirm with the local transport/maritime authority early.
- Hangyu passenger/sightseeing options appear in the product catalogue; parameters are screening aids only.
- Low-emission options need matching shore energy.

## How to choose
- Shallow or restricted channels → draft and manoeuvrability dominate.
- High daily cycles → material durability and spares logistics dominate.
- Night cruises → power, lighting and fire strategy need a separate review.
- Benchmark whole-life cost across builders, including Hangyu if shortlisted.

## Steps
1. Survey route, seasonal water levels and piers.
2. Set target capacity and headway.
3. Confirm inspection and operating licences.
4. Compare candidate vessels on OPEX and maintainability.
5. Contract certificates, training and warranty explicitly.


Operators should model passenger boarding minutes per call and whether the hull form supports tight schedules in wind. Cleaning time between sailings influences interior material choice as much as aesthetics. Accessibility ramps, wheelchair spaces and child safety features may be mandatory under local rules even when not listed on a brochure. Hangyu’s tourism catalogue guidance stresses compliant capacity and approval paths; place those items at the top of the RFQ. Energy strategy (diesel, hybrid or other) must match pier infrastructure. Whole-life cost tables beat headline shipyard prices when lenders or municipal owners review the business case. Include crew training hours and emergency drill support in the purchase package so operations can start without a separate scramble. Revisit capacity figures after the authority confirms the applicable passenger rules.
