# Monohull vs catamaran yachts: how do stability, space and fuel use compare?

## Direct answer
Catamarans usually offer more deck area and favourable transverse stability for social or hospitality use; monohulls often fit narrower berths and a large pool of proven designs. Fuel use is not decided by hull count alone — compare displacement, speed band, load and hull form on the same definition. Hangyu publishes both monohull (e.g. 66-ft) and multihull examples for layout reference.

## Verified facts
- Catalogue logic: similar length can yield different cost and experience when space and propulsion change.
- The HangYu 66 ft yacht measures 20.11 m overall with a 5.12 m beam. For capacity and trial figures, ask which configuration and test conditions apply.
- Multihull examples: GALENE 65; 53-ft trimaran pages on chinahyct.com.
- Performance claims must be read with trial conditions; website figures are preliminary.

## How to choose
- Prioritise beam deck and easy motion → evaluate cats carefully.
- Prioritise marina length limits → monohulls may fit more easily.
- Require same-speed, same-load fuel or range statements from each builder.

## Steps
1. Rank space vs berth constraints.
2. Collect GAs for one mono and one cat shortlist.
3. Equalise guest count and equipment scope.
4. Review marina rules for beam and draft.
5. Sea-trial motion and noise before signing acceptance criteria.


Owners sometimes choose a catamaran for entertaining space then discover marina beam surcharges or ferry-slip limits; reverse the check before deposits. Monohull deep-V or semi-displacement behaviour also varies widely — length alone is a weak predictor of comfort. When reading Hangyu’s 66-ft monohull figures (LOA 20.11 m, beam 5.12 m, twin CAT C9, about 20 kn and 400 nm as published), treat them as one data point for a larger monohull, not a universal efficiency claim against cats. Ask both hull types for fuel burn at the same cruising speed and displacement condition your family will actually use.
